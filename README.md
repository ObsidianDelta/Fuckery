# Fuckery

A road usually runs where a path already was. Somebody walked that line first because it
was the easiest way through the trees. Enough people walked it that it packed down, and
once it was packed down it really was the easiest way, so more people walked it. Then
somebody paved it. Then the houses went up along the paving, and now the route a person
takes to work is settled by a track worn in by people who are all dead.

Nothing in that needed a plan. At each stage the shape came out of the conditions, and
then the shape it made became part of the conditions for the next stage.

That is the whole idea here.

Start with what a thing is not allowed to do. A water molecule cannot bond at any old
angle. A person cannot be in two places. An institution cannot spend money it does not
have. Restrictions like that cut an enormous space of things that could happen down to a
much smaller space of things that can, and out of the smaller space something forms and
holds.

Then comes the part most explanations skip past. The thing that formed does not just sit
there being an outcome. It turns into a restriction in its own right. The crystal's six
arms change where the next water molecules are able to land. The road changes where the
next house goes. The habit changes what tomorrow feels like it is for. The conditions are
different now, so what forms next forms under different conditions, and that changes the
conditions again.

Structure forms under constraint. The structure becomes constraint. Round and round, and
it never comes back to where it started, because every pass runs on top of what the last
pass left behind.

That loop is the subject of everything in this repository. The formal name for it in this
work is **Fuckari**. The framework built around it is called Fuckery, which is deliberate,
and which I explain a few paragraphs down.

If this does its job, by the end you will be able to watch the same loop run in a
snowflake, in one of your own habits, in a company, and in a codebase — and you will be
able to say what would have to show up for it to be wrong. That second half matters more
than the first. Anything that fits everything explains nothing, so a fair amount of the
work here goes into what the framework is forbidden to say, including the flattering
things it would love to say about itself.

You do not need any philosophy for this, and you do not need any physics. You need to be
willing to look at something ordinary for longer than usual.

A framework for thinking about constraint, emergence, and what it takes for structure
to form and hold. Written to be used, argued with, and forked.

## Read this before anything else

There is a lot of dead end in here. Some of it is me working something out in public
and not getting there. There are also, I think, some diamonds in the rough, and I would
rather publish the rough with them than polish the whole thing into something that
sounds finished when it isn't.

This is a metacognitive toolkit — a set of tools for how to think. I built it because I
needed it for other problems I was working on. The Constraint Protocol is one of those
problems; this is the thinking that made that protocol take the shape it did.

I am not an ontologist and do not claim to be one. Take what is useful. Leave the rest.

## The name

It is called Fuckery so that nobody can genuflect at it, including me. A framework that
sounds authoritative stops getting questioned, and this one needs to keep getting
questioned.

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Fuckery** | The framework — this document and the thinking in it. |
| **Fuckari** | The formal term for the loop it describes: *emergent, top-down-causal, resonance-constraint*, compressed into one word. The mechanism. |
| **Fuckarian** | A being constituted by its assumptions — constrained by them in the same act that lets it think at all. |

## Where to start

The canon is 63,500 bytes. Nobody is expected to read that, and most people should not
begin there at all. Three ways in, shortest first. Each one is complete on its own terms,
and stopping after any of them is a normal thing to do.

**Four minutes — the idea, in one object.**
[The Snowflake — Fuckari in Action](https://jeyanandan.com/blog/snowflake-fuckari-in-action/).
Read the opening, down to the line "Let's watch the loop run," and you will have the loop
in plain language, drawn on a thing you have seen a few million of. That is the four
minutes. If you keep going, the rest is the framework run against a physical object whose
physics nobody disputes. Twenty-eight steps, one move each, with the places it breaks
down named at the end. The full post is about a twenty-five minute read and it is the
best single thing to send someone.

**Twenty minutes — the spine.** In [`FUCKERY.md`](FUCKERY.md), read §1, §2 and §3 and
then stop. §1 Thesis is one paragraph: the whole argument compressed until it barely
breathes, and it will look dense on first pass — that is expected, come back to it after
§2. §2 The Invariant Core is the four commitments everything else stands on, stated
plainly. §3 Primitives is the three things I do not derive from anything else. Those
three sections are the argument. The remaining fifteen are what happens when you push it.

**The whole thing.** **[`FUCKERY.md`](FUCKERY.md)** is the whole thing — v0.51, dated
2026-09-10. Eighteen sections, 540 lines. It is a workbench rather than a book, and it
reads better if you treat it as one: §13 is the dead ends and the things I rejected, §14
is the ten open problems held open on purpose, §16 is the change log showing what each
version broke in the one before it.

## What this is not

It is not peer-reviewed. It has had no external adversarial review. It is not a doctrine
and it does not tell you what anything means.

It also forbids things on purpose. It does not explain why there is something it is like
to be a system that runs this loop — and it equally forbids the opposite claim, that the
question is an illusion. Ten open problems are held open deliberately, each with its
forbidden overclaim attached. That list is the honest part.

## Provenance

Every claim is marked, in spirit if not in tag, as one of: **invariant** (axioms),
**derived** (definitions and consequences), **speculative** (open problems), **illustrative**
(the snowflake, the compression analogy), or **reasoning trail** (the path of inquiry).

Reasoning trails are first-class here. The path by which a claim was reached is not
scaffolding to be thrown away once the claim stands.
